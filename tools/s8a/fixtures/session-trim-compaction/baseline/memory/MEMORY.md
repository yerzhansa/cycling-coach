## General Coaching Notes

### Pacing — Rolling/Climbing Sections
- Agreed to hold a steady endurance effort on climbs rather than surging; breathing conversational, cadence comfortable through the middle hours of rides. (athlete, 1998-07-06)
- Final riser has felt harder than usual on multiple occasions; attributed to headwind and fatigue from a long day on feet, not a fitness decline. (athlete, 1998-07-06)

### Wind / River Stretch
- Wind frequently picks up on the exposed stretch along the river. (athlete, 1998-07-06)
- Strategy: tuck lower on the bars; rotate snack schedule earlier to maintain energy through the final hour and avoid fading. (coach, 1998-07-06)
- Monitor hands and shoulder comfort after longer stints in the lower position — comfort on the bike is the first thing to slip when weekly volume steps up. (coach, 1998-07-06)

### Back-to-Back Ride Recovery
- Mild leg heaviness on day 2 of back-to-back rides clears after ~20 minutes of easy spinning — normal response at this point in the build, no adjustment needed yet. (athlete, 1998-07-06)
- Rule agreed: if heaviness lingers past the first 30 minutes, shorten the second day rather than push through it. (coach, 1998-07-06)

### Fueling Protocol (rides over 2 hours)
- 1 bottle per hour. (coach, 1998-07-06)
- A bite of something every 30 minutes. (coach, 1998-07-06)
- Slightly bigger breakfast on mornings with sustained climbing work. (coach, 1998-07-06)
- Practice the fueling routine on easy days first so nothing is new or distracting on longer weekend rides. (coach, 1998-07-06)

### Recovery Spin Routing
- Route starting from the park side of town has fewer junctions, making it easier to keep effort genuinely easy. (athlete, 1998-07-06)
- All recovery spins to start from the park side of town going forward. (coach, 1998-07-06)
- Athlete confirmed breathing stayed relaxed the whole way around on this route — the target signal for easy days. (athlete, 1998-07-06)
- Easy/recovery days to be protected even when the schedule gets busy. (coach, 1998-07-06)
